## New summaries

### Hassan et al. — Hybrid Quantum-Classical NLP Classification with Compact Semantic Representations (arXiv:2609.10089v1)

- **Problem:** Sentence embeddings are 768/384-dim but near-term QML circuits encode only a handful of features (one angle per qubit). How much semantic information can be compressed into a quantum-compatible representation before classification collapses — and how much is the *classical* compression vs any *quantum* contribution?
- **Method:** Hybrid pipeline: frozen `all-MiniLM-L6-v2` embeddings → dimensionality reduction (PCA vs supervised NCA/LDA) → $R_y$ angle encoding → 5-qubit, 2-layer hardware-efficient VQC (20 params, ring CNOT) → readout of five $\langle Z_j\rangle$ + five $\langle Z_jZ_{j+1}\rangle$ into a classical softmax. Evaluated on TREC (5,452/500) with a leakage-free cross-validation protocol; small restaurant-sentiment set as a second case.
- **Key results:** PCA shows a severe bottleneck — 3/4/5/8 dims retain only 8.2/10.2/11.9/16.4% variance with 50.3/51.2/57.9/63.4% accuracy (83.8% at full 384 dims). Supervised reduction is far more efficient: at **5 dims LDA 85.3%, NCA 83.1%**, matching/beating the full 384-dim classical MLP baseline (85.1%). Proper leakage-free fitting dropped NCA 7.8 pp and LDA 4.6 pp vs leak-prone fitting. Explained variance is an unreliable proxy for accuracy.
- **Limitations / bottlenecks:** "Preliminary"; critically, **no matched quantum-vs-classical comparison on the *same* compressed features** — so gains are attributable to classical reduction, not demonstrated quantum expressibility. Simulation only, ≤8 qubits, linear reductions only. LDA capped at $C-1$ dims.
- **Why it matters to me:** Strong fit — QML encoding/data-loading bottleneck + QML-on-classical-data (real NLP text) + quantum+NLP, with honest methodology (refuses to claim quantum advantage, insists on matched baselines). Caveat: the quantum-vs-classical head-to-head is deferred, so it demonstrates a compression interface more than a quantum benefit; no finance/fraud angle.

### Tang et al. — Characterizing Privacy Risks of QML with Emergent Quantum-Native Access (arXiv:2609.05702v2)

- **Problem:** Membership-inference-attack (MIA) studies against QML assume a purely *classical* adversary receiving classical outputs. In an emergent quantum-native world (programmable measurements or pre-measurement state access via quantum networks), does a quantum-capable adversary gain a privacy-attack advantage, and does quantum-information access enable stronger MIAs?
- **Method:** Formalizes MIAs against QNNs as a LiRA-style membership game across three access regimes — classical outcome-only (C), programmable Pauli measurement (M), full pre-measurement state (Q) — each with a $k$-shot budget. Uses quantum hypothesis testing (TV distance, Helstrom) to derive optimal advantages and a membership-conditioned state discrepancy $\Delta(x)$. Empirics: 4-qubit/3-layer QNN (24 angles) in PennyLane on MNIST 0-vs-1 and 3-vs-8 (top-4 PCA features), varying shots $k$ and surrogate pairs $K$.
- **Key results:** (Thm III.1) under classicalized output, a quantum adversary gains **no** advantage (equals $\mathrm{TV}$). (Thm III.2) strict leakage hierarchy $L_C\le L_M\le L_Q$, with $L_Q=\tfrac12\|\Delta(x)\|_1$ — more quantum access ⇒ more leakage. (Thm III.3) finite-resource gap bounded by $\sqrt{2d^k/K}$. Experiments confirm the hierarchy: larger $k$ and higher-Pauli-weight measurements strictly beat classical, approaching the Q bound; the $K^{-1/2}$ bound is loose (observed gaps decay faster).
- **Limitations / bottlenecks:** Small-scale (4-qubit/24-param), noiseless sim only; MIA susceptibility is model/instance-dependent; the $\sqrt{2d^k/K}$ bound is non-tight; the strongest Q regime depends on immature quantum networking; proposed defenses (measurement/shot restriction, quantum DP) discussed but not implemented.
- **Why it matters to me:** Strong fit — rigorous proof-driven QML privacy theory (Helstrom/TV hypothesis testing, formal leakage hierarchy + sample-complexity bounds) in my "rigorous theory" and "implications of quantum computing / data privacy" areas; security/privacy framing (MIAs, DP defenses) is relevant to payments where training-data confidentiality matters, on QNNs over classical data. Caveat: a privacy characterization, not a fraud application or advantage result; toy-scale, noiseless.

### Joshi et al. — Balancing Expressivity and Overfitting in Quantum Gaussian Process Regression (arXiv:2609.09407v1)

- **Problem:** In active learning, the GPR surrogate's quality is dominated by its kernel. How does a *quantum* fidelity kernel behave as the GPR covariance inside an active-learning loop, and how sensitive is it to regularization? The claim: beyond the known large-scale exponential concentration, overfitting can collapse GPR even at *small* scale.
- **Method:** Active-learning loop (query max-variance point, retrain) with quantum GPR surrogating $f(x)=x\sin(x)$ on $[0,2\pi]$. Fidelity kernel $|\langle\phi(x)|\phi(x')\rangle|^2$ with "Simple" (linear entanglement) and "Chebyshev" (circular, $\cos^{-1}$ preprocessing) feature maps; $N=2$ (and 4) qubits, $L=2$ layers. Sweeps signal variance $\tau^2$ and noise variance $\lambda^2$ over $\{0.01,0.1,0.5,1,10\}$ (25 combos), 30 points, 10 AL iterations, MSE on 20 test points; SPSA on negative log marginal likelihood, Qiskit AerSimulator into scikit-learn GPR.
- **Key results:** Four regimes: (1) **overfitting** when $\tau^2>\lambda^2$ (Chebyshev) — covariance goes diagonal, fails even on training data (MSE blows up to 155–10466); (2) **limited expressivity** for Simple at $N=2$ (fixed by $N=4$); (3) **underfitting** when $\tau^2<\lambda^2$ — covariance ≈ all-ones, surrogate locks to global mean; (4) **balanced** at larger $\tau^2$ with moderate $\lambda^2$ (best MSE ~0.003–0.01). MSE alone can't distinguish failure modes — the kernel matrix must be inspected. Classical RBF showed only underfitting.
- **Limitations / bottlenecks:** By design small/low-dim (1D target, $N=2$–4 qubits, $L=2$), explicitly *not* the quantum-advantage regime; good hyperparameters must balance against covariance magnitude (doesn't transfer trivially); simulator only; no theorem — qualitative empirical study on one synthetic task.
- **Why it matters to me:** Fits the QML-bottlenecks axis — quantum-kernel concentration/overfitting framed through classical statistical-learning tools (GPR regularization, bias–variance), with a concrete diagnostic (inspect the kernel matrix, not just MSE) and the "engineered noise can help near-term devices" angle. Partial fit: no finance/fraud, no formal theory, single synthetic 1D regression — intuition/diagnostic more than rigorous theory or a classical-data advantage result.
