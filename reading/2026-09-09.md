### Kerenidis — Scalable Quantum Machine Learning: Trainability, Expressivity and Efficiency (arXiv:2607.24014v2)

- **Problem:** Parameterized quantum circuits for deep learning face three simultaneous obstacles the paper argues have blocked a genuinely *scalable* architecture: (1) barren plateaus; (2) no guarantee the learned function class is classically hard (trainable small-DLA circuits tend to be classically simulable); (3) prohibitive circuit evaluations per gradient step (naive parameter-shift is $O(n^2)$). It frames five challenges (C1 data loading, C2 efficient PQC layers, C3 no exponential barren plateaus, C4 sub-linear gradient cost, C5 genuine classical hardness) and claims to resolve all five, leaving practical usefulness (C6) explicitly open.
- **Method:** Theory paper (no experiments). Two fermionic architectures from particle-number-preserving RBS gates interleaved with single-qubit $R_z$ phases: the **unitary brick-wall** (nearest-neighbor, depth $2n$, $\tfrac{3}{2}n^2$ params) and **unitary butterfly** (all-to-all, depth $2\log n$, $\tfrac{3}{2}n\log n$ params). The $R_z$ gates lift the dynamical Lie algebra from $\mathfrak{so}(n)$ to $\mathfrak{u}(n)$. Data loading via a non-Gaussian magic-state encoding; particle number $k$ is a tunable dial trading classical hardness vs training cost. Main contribution: a multi-layer parallel parameter-shift rule costing only $2k$ evaluations per layer. Trainability proved via Weingarten/Haar second moments; hardness rests on Fermion Sampling.
- **Key results:** Two-body correlator gradient variance $\Theta(k^3/n^5)$ (polynomial in $n$) → no exponential barren plateaus (vs $\Theta(1/\binom{n}{k})$ for orthogonal circuits). Parallel PSR computes all gradients in $4kn$ / $4k\log n$ evaluations — a factor $n/k$ reduction ($17\times$ at $n{=}1024,k{=}60$; $68\times$ at $n{=}4096$). Hardness ladder in $k$: $O(\log n)$ classically simulable → $k=\Theta(n)$ full average-case Fermion Sampling. Generalization $O(\sqrt{T\log m/m})$ scales with parameter count, not Hilbert-space dimension.
- **Limitations / bottlenecks:** No empirical demonstration (guarantees "necessary but not sufficient"). Butterfly's sharp rate is conditional on an unproven 2-design conjecture. Average-case hardness proven only at $k=\Theta(n)$, not the $k=60$ operating point. Fixed-body expectations are classically tractable at any $k$, so hardness requires the deployed output to be the *sample set*. A v1 claim of a hard trainable expectation-value pipeline was refuted (Amidi) and removed.
- **Why it matters to me:** Strong fit — the definitive "QML bottlenecks + rigorous theory" paper on trainability, gradient cost, and the trainability-vs-hardness tension. Explicitly targets generative risk modeling and low-body-return RL (naming portfolio construction/execution), so payments-adjacent, though not fraud-specific and entirely theoretical.

### Alcántara et al. — A Representation-Theoretic Framework for Characterizing Barren Plateaus (arXiv:2609.04462v1)

- **Problem:** DLA-based barren-plateau theory gives exact variance formulas only when the initial state *or* observable lives inside the circuit's dynamical Lie algebra — excluding higher-order observables, encoded/entangled states, and Hamiltonians whose content spills outside the algebra. Is that restriction intrinsic or an artifact of the math language?
- **Method:** Recasts trainability as harmonic analysis on compact Lie groups. Assuming the deep ensemble is a unitary 2-design over group $G$, decomposes operator space into irreducible-representation channels; Schur orthogonality makes cross-terms vanish so the second moment splits into non-negative per-irrep contributions. Yields exact variance + bounds (Thm 8), a Schatten-norm representation-independent bound (Thm 10). Validated on the 1D ANNNI model across three ansätze (DLAs $\mathfrak{su}(2^n)$, $\mathfrak{su}(2)^n$, $\mathfrak{u}(1)^{2n-1}$) in PennyLane.
- **Key results:** $\mathrm{Var}[\ell]=\sum_{\zeta\neq\tau}\mathcal C_\zeta(\rho,O)$; prior DLA theory recovered as the adjoint-representation special case. Fully expressive $\mathfrak{su}(2^n)$ → exponential suppression (barren plateau); local $\mathfrak{su}(2)^n$ → extensive (linear-in-$n$) variance for any separable *or* GHZ state; commuting $\mathfrak{u}(1)^{2n-1}$ → system-size-independent lower bound $\ge 1/2^6$. A single-irrep certificate (Cor 8.1) proves nonvanishing variance without computing the full variance. Numerics over $10^4$ realizations match analytics.
- **Limitations / bottlenecks:** Rests entirely on the unitary 2-design assumption (asymptotic deep regime); finite-depth corrections and away-from-2-design landscapes are open. Applying Thm 8 needs the explicit irrep decomposition (problem-specific). Demonstrated on one Hamiltonian.
- **Why it matters to me:** Strong fit on QML-bottlenecks + rigorous theory. Proof-driven, unifies prior BP theory, and gives an actionable design principle (symmetry-restrict the ansatz to protect trainability) plus a practical lower-bound certificate. Caveats: physics observable (ANNNI energy), not classical-data/finance, and 2-design assumption limits shallow-circuit applicability.

### Zhang et al. — A Theory of Finite-Noise Optima and Generalization in QML (arXiv:2608.24229v1)

- **Problem:** Moderate quantum noise sometimes *reduces* test error — a non-monotonic effect unexplained by weak-noise perturbation or strong-noise collapse. What sets the optimal noise level and why does the benefit vanish?
- **Method:** Statistical learning theory via a noise-order surrogate: the noisy predictor as a binomial-weighted ensemble over predictors grouped by number of noise occurrences. Loss splits into bias + a variance/regularization term. Central quantity: noise-order purity $s_2(\beta,g)\approx e^{-2\beta g}I_0(2\beta g)$ controlling effective complexity $d_{\rm eff}$ and the generalization gap. Tested on a 4-qubit re-uploading regressor (diabetes dataset) and 8–9-qubit QGNNs (QM9/PCQM4Mv2 HOMO–LUMO), across depolarizing/bit-flip/phase-damping/amplitude-damping channels (PennyLane density-matrix sim).
- **Key results:** Across all channels, training loss rises monotonically but **test loss dips below noiseless then rises** — a finite-noise optimum; the gap follows the $s_2$ decay and $d_{\rm eff}\propto s_2$. Three gradient regimes; benefit *precedes* trainability collapse. Optimum $s^\star=\mathrm{SNR}/(\mathrm{SNR}+d_{\rm eff}^{(0)}/n_{\rm train})$: more training data shifts the optimum toward zero noise (non-monotonicity nearly gone at $n_{\rm train}=1000$). "Noise programming" can push a model toward the optimum but only *adds* effective noise.
- **Limitations / bottlenecks:** Assumes independent gate-level noise (correlated/finite-shot open). Relies on a surrogate + local linearization, not the physical model. $d_{\rm eff}\propto s_2$ validated only numerically. Small scale (4–9 qubits). Noise programming is directional (can't undo native noise past the optimum).
- **Why it matters to me:** Strong fit — QML-bottleneck (noise/generalization) with rigorous statistical-learning framing and a closed-form optimum, reframing noise as a tunable regularizer. On classical data (clinical/molecular), aligning with QML-on-classical-data, though no finance/fraud angle.

### Kamisoyama et al. — Double Descent in Quantum Kernel Ridge Regression (arXiv:2604.17202v2)

- **Problem:** Double descent is well studied classically but underexplored in QML; prior quantum work showed the peak in *ridgeless* kernel regression but no explicit predictive test-risk formula. Can one rigorously derive the test risk for quantum kernel *ridge* regression (QKRR, $\lambda>0$) and quantify how regularization suppresses the peak?
- **Method:** Maps QKRR to classical ridge regression in the normalized Pauli basis (ambient dim $4^n$), defines an effective feature map of dimension $p=\mathrm{rank}(\Sigma)$, and applies deterministic equivalents from random matrix theory (resolvent + derivative trick). Validated by statevector simulation (HEA, TPA) on synthetic teacher–student (3,5 qubits) and Fashion-MNIST binary (3 qubits).
- **Key results:** Theorem 1 gives an explicit test-risk DE with effective regularization $\kappa_\lambda$ and effective degrees of freedom $\eta_\lambda$; the peak is a variance divergence at $\eta_\lambda\to1$ (interpolation threshold $p=N_{\rm tr}$). As $\lambda\downarrow0$, variance grows as $\lambda^{-1/2}$; explicit $\lambda>0$ suppresses it; ridgeless $\kappa_0>0$ acts as implicit regularization. DE matches empirical MSE in peak location and magnitude; agreement improves with larger effective dimension.
- **Limitations / bottlenecks:** Pointwise at fixed $\lambda>0$; benign-overfitting/scaling-laws regime uncovered. Assumes a noise-free kernel matrix — ignores shot noise and quantum kernel concentration, so many-qubit scalability unaddressed. Needs population covariance estimated from large auxiliary datasets. Notes highly expressive Haar-like circuits give flat spectra (no inductive bias), making quantum generalization advantage hard.
- **Why it matters to me:** Strong fit — rigorous statistical-learning theory (RMT, bias–variance, effective DoF) on a QML bottleneck (kernel generalization), with candid discussion of kernel concentration and when quantum kernels can help. Touches classical data (Fashion-MNIST). No finance/fraud application.

### Tekeli — The ZZ feature map induces a signless-Laplacian metric: a closed-form classical surrogate for quantum kernel regression (arXiv:2608.29422v1)

> Sourcing caveat: the converted Markdown contains only the arXiv abstract/landing page, not the 30-page body. Claims below are grounded in the abstract/metadata only; derivations, tables, and figures could not be verified.

- **Problem:** Quantum kernels lose their advantage once encoding bandwidth is tuned, resembling RBF kernels — but prior analytical support covered only separable circuits and entangling ones qualitatively. What classical kernel is an *entangling* ZZ feature map actually computing?
- **Method:** Analytical derivation (verified against simulation). Proves that in the small-bandwidth regime the induced kernel is, to leading order, an anisotropic Gaussian kernel with metric $M=I+\pi^2 Q$, where $Q$ is the signless Laplacian of the entanglement graph; the quadratic structure persists at every depth as a Fubini–Study pullback. Anisotropy depends on a phase convention (unshifted → identity metric, explaining prior isotropic RBF resemblance). Verified on path/cycle/complete graphs; empirical comparison on two near-infrared spectroscopy benchmarks.
- **Key results (from abstract):** Derivation matches simulation to relative error $<10^{-3}$. Paired 95% bootstrap interval contains zero in 18/20 cells; typical relative test-error difference 2.7%; same preprocessing pipeline selected in 96% of splits. The failure regime begins at the same bandwidth on both datasets and adds no robust predictive value; restricting to the classical regime improves mean test error 3.8%. Conclusion: the quantum circuit can be removed without detectable predictive loss.
- **Limitations / bottlenecks:** Leading-order small-bandwidth result; a failure regime exists (argued non-predictive). Only two spectroscopy datasets and the ZZ map. Anisotropy hinges on a phase-convention choice. Body text unavailable for verification.
- **Why it matters to me:** Strong fit on QML-bottlenecks/when-quantum-doesn't-help + rigorous theory: a dequantization-style result showing a bandwidth-tuned entangling quantum kernel equals a specific classical anisotropic Gaussian kernel. Domain (near-infrared spectroscopy) is off my payments focus, but the lesson — verify what your quantum kernel is really computing before claiming advantage — transfers directly.

### Appel — Fractal dimension predicts quantum kernel collapse in angle-encoded data (arXiv:2609.00475v1)

- **Problem:** Angle-encoded quantum fidelity kernels on tabular data suffer exponential concentration — as qubits grow, $K$ collapses to the identity, killing every downstream method. The usual PCA-95% recipe can already feed a *dead* kernel. Is there an a-priori, classically computable qubit budget before collapse?
- **Method:** Proposes the correlation fractal dimension $D_2$ as the qubit ceiling $q^*=\lceil D_2\rceil$ and FD-ASE to select which columns to encode. $D_2$/FD-ASE estimated classically on a geometry sample; collapse judged by kernel diagnostics (near/far/mean-off-diagonal) with an explicit "alive rule." Nine datasets, statevector sim, RBF control, feature-map/bandwidth/packing ablations, synthetic known-width family, and IBM hardware (ibm_fez, 256 shots).
- **Key results:** $\lceil D_2\rceil$ sits at/next to the empirical knee on clean cases (breast $D_2{=}3$ = knee 3, vs PCA-95% asking 10). Classical RBF stays alive as $q$ grows → collapse is a quantum-map property. Budget is map- and bandwidth-dependent (a 2nd $ZZ$ layer dies earlier; product-$Z$/IQP overshoot; smaller bandwidth delays collapse). Packing two PCs/qubit stays alive at fractal $q$ but stacking PCA-95%-scale counts kills it. Hardware reproduces the live kernel at fractal width (MAE 0.012–0.021) and is diagonal past it; at $n=16$ FD-ASE is the only selector whose device kernel ranks neighbors above strangers.
- **Limitations / bottlenecks:** Box-count $D_2$ needs enough samples/scales and is unreliable when $E\gg\log N$. FD-ASE selects original columns (misses oblique signal) and isn't alive on every table. Alive rule is operational, not a hypothesis test. Ceiling is tight only for one-layer $ZZ$ at $c=1$. Single hardware backend, $n\le16$, no error mitigation.
- **Why it matters to me:** Strong fit — targets the core kernel-concentration bottleneck with a classically-cheap a-priori mitigation, squarely on QML-on-classical-tabular-data, blending classical statistical learning (intrinsic dimension) with quantum kernels and honest about where they fail. Finance/fraud relevance is indirect (UCI tables), but the diagnostic-and-budget method transfers to any tabular fraud/anomaly QSVM pipeline.

### Matsuura & Johri — Discretization-Aware Fine-Tuning for QML with Chemical Foundation Models (arXiv:2609.03220v1)

- **Problem:** Bit-bit (basis) encoding compresses features into short binary strings, causing cross-class collisions (different-label samples → same bit-string) that are provably unclassifiable. How does upstream representation, not circuit design, govern QML competitiveness under this bottleneck?
- **Method:** DAFT — adapts a pretrained ChemBERTa-77M with a differentiable soft collision loss (Gaussian-kernel softmax over bins) penalizing opposite-class pairs sharing a quantization bin. Bit-bit pipeline (PCA → MI bit-allocation → quantization → basis state) into a bipartite entangling classifier at $n_q\in\{4,6,8,10\}$ (progressive subnet init to avoid barren plateaus). Info-controlled protocol vs logistic regression on the *identical* bit-string, on BBBP (scaffold split, 5 seeds).
- **Key results:** At $n_q=4$, collisions drop 11,544 → ≈1 and QML accuracy rises +11–13 pp. Headline at $n_q=10$: **without DAFT classical lr-bit beats QML 0.821 vs 0.761** (all 5 seeds); **with DAFT the comparison reverses**, QML 0.883 vs 0.855 (+2.8 pp, $p=0.026$), driven by a differential DAFT gain (+12.2 pp QML vs +3.4 pp classical). Independently corroborated by the Fisher condition (+2.3 pp, $p=0.001$).
- **Limitations / bottlenecks:** Only 5 seeds; main advantage rests on $n=4$ after one seed excluded. Modest (+2.8 pp) advantage vs a *linear* baseline, specific to the bit-bit regime. Ideal statevector sim (no noise/shots). One dataset, one foundation model. Commercial framework (Red Cedar).
- **Why it matters to me:** Strong fit — targets the encoding/data-loading bottleneck with a rigorous info-controlled protocol isolating representation from quantum processing, addressing "when does QML actually beat classical?" On real classical data with an honest matched baseline; the transformer-embedding-into-register angle touches quantum+NLP. Gaps: chemistry not finance, noiseless only, fragile statistics — but the "align continuous representation to the discrete register" principle transfers to financial pipelines.

### Peng et al. — When Similarity Is Interaction-Driven: Quantum Kernels for Regime-Sensitive Learning (arXiv:2608.24631v1)

- **Problem:** In fraud/anomaly detection, similarity is often driven by sparse high-order variable interactions, not distance — two transactions can be close yet in different risk regimes. Distance-based kernels are misaligned. Can an interaction-aligned quantum kernel represent regime similarity better?
- **Method:** A controlled "thin-slab" synthetic model (labels = majority vote over block-product sign regimes) plus an interaction-driven quantum kernel from entangled Pauli-string feature maps (per-block $H^{\otimes k}$, one-body $R_Z$, and a $k$-qubit Pauli-$Z$ string encoding the block product). Fidelity kernel proven PSD, block-factorized closed form. Baselines: linear/RBF/Laplacian/polynomial + InterFea (a strong control given the planted products). Kernel ridge regression, 10 seeds, on synthetic (3rd–8th order) and two real FDB fraud benchmarks (Credit Card, IEEE-CIS). Exact classical simulation.
- **Key results:** Quantum kernel best across balanced/imbalanced synthetic (e.g. ~0.73 acc vs ~0.50 for generic kernels at $(20,4,5)$; F1 up to 0.534). Scaling: best acc/F1 at 6th (0.706/0.412) and 8th order (0.695/0.395); ~67%/54% F1 gains over the strongest generic kernel. Real data: Credit Card Fraud best F1 0.472 vs Poly2 0.348 (~36% gain), beating Poly2 in all 10 runs; IEEE-CIS **second** behind Laplacian. Central claim: performance depends on feature-map–target *alignment*, not Hilbert-space dimension; advantage is representational (classically reproducible), not computational.
- **Limitations / bottlenecks:** Synthetic generator matched to the feature map (block partition known a priori). Credit Card test split has only 75 fraud cases. Exact simulation only (no shot/device noise). $O(n^2)$ kernel storage. No computational advantage claimed; IEEE-CIS knocks it to second.
- **Why it matters to me:** Strong fit — QML-on-classical-data + finance/fraud (real FDB benchmarks), engaging *when* quantum kernels help via inductive-bias alignment with statistical rigor (PSD proof, learning curves, imbalance). Honest for the "where QML doesn't fail" angle: gains are real but representational, classically reproducible, and partly matched by a classical engineered-feature baseline.

### Faryad — Quantum Kernel k-Means for Credit-Card Fraud Detection: A Controlled Benchmark (arXiv:2608.15718v1)

- **Problem:** Does a quantum fidelity kernel genuinely help credit-card fraud detection, or do "advantages" come from flawed benchmarking (weak classical baselines, select-and-report on same data, single splits)?
- **Method:** Quantum kernel $k$-means on the real ULB dataset (284,807 tx, 0.173% fraud); registers of 2/4/8/12 qubits; budget-matched held-out protocol (dev seeds 0–3, report on 30 disjoint seeds 100–129), paired $t$-tests + 95% CIs. Quantum search over 264 feature-map configs vs classical 105 kernel configs; ARI on enriched resamples. Qiskit Aer exact statevectors + a finite-shot repeat.
- **Key results:** No robust advantage — sign flips with register size (worse at 4q, better at 8q $+0.0086$, indistinguishable at 12q); all effect sizes $<0.013$ ARI. The 8-qubit "advantage" is a search-budget artefact (drawing 20 vs 264 configs → quantum leads only 34%). A single classical bandwidth moves performance up to 0.163 ARI (~14× the largest quantum gap). More qubits hurt via kernel concentration (mean off-diagonal 0.80→0.51 from 2→14 qubits). Finite-shot reproduces exact ARI. Below ~5% fraud clustering collapses; anomaly-scoring survives but quantum (AUROC 0.949) still no edge over classical (0.955).
- **Limitations / bottlenecks:** Single dataset (PCA features may suit classical kernels); no trainable feature maps; noiseless sim (called conservative); unrealistic 15% enrichment for clustering; ignores asymptotic kernel-eval speedup ($\sim5\times10^7$ circuits at $N=10^4$).
- **Why it matters to me:** Strong fit — QML-on-classical-data on finance/fraud, fundamentally about QML bottlenecks (kernel concentration, bandwidth dominance). Its search-budget ablation converting a "significant" advantage into an artefact is a superb cautionary template for evaluating quantum fraud classifiers — exactly the "where does QML not fail / why apparent wins are illusory" analysis I value.

### Kumar et al. — Dynamic Entanglement-Weighted Pruning for Quantum Federated Unlearning in Supply-Chain Risk Prediction (arXiv:2608.17069v1)

- **Problem:** Federated variational quantum classifiers must honor GDPR right-to-erasure, but full retraining is wasteful and it's unclear which circuit parameters carry a client's influence (entanglement spreads it non-locally).
- **Method:** Entanglement-Weighted Pruning (EWP): score each parameter by the product of its diagonal quantum Fisher information on the forgetting client (parameter-shift) and a structural entanglement weight (mean concurrence over CNOT-coupled pairs); prune below threshold, then short fine-tune on retained clients. 4-qubit/3-layer data-re-uploading ansatz (24 params), FedAvg over 5 non-IID supply-chain clients, noiseless sim, 3 seeds. Baselines: full retrain (oracle), fine-tune only, random/Fisher-only/entanglement-only pruning.
- **Key results:** EWP reaches accuracy $0.837$/AUROC $0.907$, statistically indistinguishable from the retrain oracle ($p=0.28$), with better forgetting and membership-inference scores, at a $16.4\times$ speed-up (3.96s vs 65s). Random/Fisher-only/entanglement-only all collapse to ~0.40–0.47 → both signals jointly necessary. Forgetting weakens as heterogeneity rises.
- **Limitations / bottlenecks:** Only diagonal QFIM; noiseless, 4-qubit/24-param/3-seed scale; membership result is attacker-specific, not a certified guarantee; synthetic data. Threshold sweep (Table 7, pre-fine-tuning) shows 0.344 accuracy at $\tau=0.2$, so the headline depends heavily on the fine-tuning recovery pass.
- **Why it matters to me:** Strong fit — quantum federated learning on a finance/commerce-adjacent tabular task (supply-chain risk), foregrounding privacy/GDPR (relevant to payments), and engaging QML bottlenecks (trainability, entanglement, parameter-shift cost) via quantum Fisher information. Read as a mechanism demonstration, not evidence QML wins (no classical unlearning baseline, tiny scale, fine-tuning doing much of the lifting).

### Aizpurua et al. — Quantum Large Language Models via Tensor Network Disentanglers (arXiv:2410.17397v2)

- **Problem:** How to inject quantum resources into a *pretrained* LLM without degrading accuracy? Classical tensor-network compression has a bond-dimension ceiling; prior quantum-language work handled only simple models.
- **Method:** Replace each weight matrix in attention/MLP layers with VQC + MPO + VQC. Tensorize $W$ to an MPO, compute two disentangling circuits (MERA-style) that push entanglement into the circuits, truncate the residual MPO to low bond dimension. Circuits initialized to leave the layer unchanged (so the hybrid exactly reproduces the baseline); a trainable Cayley unitary adapter (init identity) is fine-tuned with distillation to surpass it. Demonstrated by replacing SmolLM2's 10th-layer attention weight ((576,192) → (10,8) qubits); perplexity on WikiText; partial execution on IBM Heron.
- **Key results:** Disentangling alone changes PPL by ≤0.003%. Residual MPO truncated to bond dim 1 = **~36 params** (vs 110,592 in $W$) at only +0.26% PPL; undisentangled truncation costs +9.7% (~30× worse). Training the Cayley adapter lowers PPL up to ~1.6% below baseline. Hardware proof-of-concept answers "9×6" with 2-qubit disentanglers on IBM Heron.
- **Limitations / bottlenecks:** Only a single layer replaced. Classical-memory saving realized only if circuits run on a QPU (disentanglers carry ≥ as many params as $W$). Hardware limited to 2-qubit gates. Disentangling accuracy saturates below unity at $\chi=1$. Real orthogonal gates only; not run to convergence. Explicitly disclaims any inference-speed quantum advantage.
- **Why it matters to me:** Strong fit — quantum + NLP / quantum transformers, targeting the data/parameter-loading bottleneck with a construction guaranteeing the hybrid never underperforms the classical baseline (a clean answer to bad-initialization/barren-plateau worries), with honest numbers and a real QPU demo. Weaker on finance/fraud (no financial data) and formal theory (constructive, not a proven separation).
